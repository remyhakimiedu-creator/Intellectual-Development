# Part 1: "Why that post felt like it was written about you"

**Series:** Intensity, Sensitivity, Giftedness and Neurodivergence (6 parts)
**Core message:** Resonance is not diagnosis. A checklist that fits everyone can't tell you anything specific about anyone.
**Formats:** (A) a Threads/Instagram carousel, (B) a 60–75s TikTok/Reels script, (C) a caption.
**Status:** Draft v1. Rewrite the lines in your own voice before posting.

---

## A. Carousel (9 slides)

**Slide 1: Hook**
> 7 signs your brain is wired differently.
> Count how many apply to you. Keep the number.

**Slide 2: The checklist**
> 1. You replay conversations hours later, analysing what you said.
> 2. Unfairness bothers you more than it seems to bother others.
> 3. Loud, crowded places drain you faster than you expect.
> 4. You hold yourself to standards you'd never expect of someone else.
> 5. You feel things deeply but often keep it to yourself.
> 6. You get absorbed in things that interest you and lose track of time.
> 7. You've often felt slightly out of step with the people around you.

**Slide 3: The turn**
> Got 5 or more?
> So did almost everyone who read this.
> That's not a coincidence. The list was built that way.

**Slide 4: The experiment**
> In 1948, psychologist Bertram Forer gave his students a personality test.
> A week later, each student got a "personal" profile.
> They rated its accuracy, on average, about 4.3 out of 5.

**Slide 5: The reveal**
> Every student got the same profile.
> Most of it was copied from a newsstand astrology book.
> Lines like: "You have a tendency to be critical of yourself."
> "Disciplined and self-controlled outside, you tend to be worrisome and insecure inside."

**Slide 6: The name**
> This is the **Barnum (or Forer) effect**: we accept vague, flattering, general statements as uniquely true about us.
> Viral checklists have every ingredient:
> → vague wording
> → flattering framing ("deep", "different")
> → clinical-looking design
> → "you" language

**Slide 7: Why it hits harder now**
> Every trait on that list also appears in lists about high sensitivity, giftedness, ADHD, autism, anxiety *and* trauma.
> One trait, 4–7 possible causes.
> A list made only of shared traits can't tell them apart.

**Slide 8: But some recognition is real**
> Many adults, especially women, were missed as kids and recognised themselves online first.
> So the question isn't "Do I relate?"
> It's:
> **Since when? Where? What does it cost me?**

**Slide 9: CTA**
> Relating is where curiosity starts, not where answers end.
> Next: the Polish psychiatrist who said your breakdown might be a breakthrough. (Part 2)
> *Education, not diagnosis.*

## C. Caption

> I built that checklist to fit almost everyone, and it probably fit you.
>
> In 1948, Bertram Forer showed that people rate a generic personality description as about 86% accurate (4.3/5) when they believe it's about them. Today's "signs you're gifted / HSP / neurodivergent" posts run on the same psychology.
>
> That doesn't mean your experiences aren't real. It means a checklist can't explain *why* you have them.
>
> Three better questions: Since when? In which situations? What is it costing you?
>
> Part 2 next: Dąbrowski and the 5 overexcitabilities.
>
> *This is education, not diagnosis. If these traits are getting in the way of work, study or relationships, a qualified clinician can help you work out what's driving them.*
